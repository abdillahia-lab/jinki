Why it holds up

TO A REGULATOR · IN A DEPOSITION

A condition-of-record earns its name by what it survives — here is the scrutiny it is built to withstand.

  • A deposition or cross-examination — chain-of-custody and capture metadata (provenance, geolocation, time-stamp) travel with every dataset, producible on demand when opposing counsel asks where a finding came from.
  • A regulator or NAIC AI exam — outputs are explainable and severity-ranked, each stating how it was reached and carrying its confidence, built for review, not a black box to defend.
  • An appraisal or coverage dispute — the record states what it rests on, so imagery is one input, never the sole basis — and it augments your people, never replaces their judgment.
THE STANDING RULE

Imagery is one input, never the sole basis for a decision — the record states what it rests on, so it holds up to a regulator or in court.
