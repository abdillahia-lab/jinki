THE THESIS

Roof condition is among the largest drivers of property loss — yet it is assessed late, inconsistently, and rarely in a form that survives a deposition.

JINKI BUILDS THE RECORD THAT DOES.
”

Underwriting and claims turn on the same disputed fact — the true state of a roof. Roof-related causes drive a large share of property loss, and roof condition is among the most disputed facts in a claim (directional industry framing, not a Jinki measurement). Yet most aerial vendors return imagery and call it evidence. An image is one input. A defensible decision needs a geolocated, time-stamped record, an explainable analysis, and a chain of custody behind both — the gap Jinki closes. Instrument-grade, and narrow on purpose.

The method — instrument-grade, in three movements

CAPTURE · ANALYZE · RECORD
01Capture

Aerial thermal & visual imagery, flown inside the thermal window — bound by solar geometry, when contrast is real, not when the calendar permits.

CAPTURE GEOMETRYSCHEMATIC
02Analyze

AI surfaces thermal & structural anomalies, ranked SEV-1/2/3, each carrying a confidence value. Findings under 0.80 route to a human.

CONFIDENCE DISTRIBUTIONSCHEMATIC
03Record

A geolocated, time-stamped condition-of-record. Re-scans on cadence track degradation velocity — a living file, not a snapshot.

DEGRADATION VELOCITYSCHEMATIC

Every finding is ranked, scored, and accountable.

Severity says how much a finding matters; confidence says how far to trust the machine. Read together on one plane — and the model never decides alone where it is unsure.

< 0.80 routes to a human reviewer before it reaches the brief. Imagery is an input, not a verdict.

Why it holds up — built for a deposition, not a dashboard

DEFENSIBILITY

03Imagery is one input
